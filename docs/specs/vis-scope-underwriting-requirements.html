Document Purpose & Scope
This document outlines the theoretical architecture, workflow specifications, and structured elicitation questions for extending the Vis-Scope inspection ecosystem into an Office Edition designed specifically for Collateral Risk Analysts, Appraisal Desk Reviewers, Underwriting Managers, and Chief Credit Officers. Stakeholders are invited to review the proposed functional modules and complete the decision questionnaires in Section 5.

01 Operational Context: Field Capture vs. Office Adjudication

The existing Vis-Scope Field Client (Core v1.0) is engineered for high-mobility, touch-first jobsites—allowing inspectors and contractors to model ANSI Z765 vector floor plans, photograph defects, dictating scopes of work, and generate material takeoffs. The Office Edition serves an entirely distinct operational purpose: collateral risk validation, public record discrepancy reconciliation, cost audit, and loan/rehab decisioning.

Primary User Field Inspector, Fee Appraiser, General Contractor Collateral Underwriter, Desk Reviewer, Risk Officer
Operating Environment iPad / Android Tablet / Ruggedized Laptop on jobsite Multi-monitor Desktop Office Workstation (Chrome/Edge)

02 Architectural Topology: Monorepo Persona Routing

To eliminate schema drift and guarantee zero duplication of core calculation logic, the Office Edition will be integrated directly into the Vis-Scope codebase under a dedicated route (/office or /review) backed by role-based access controls (RBAC).

Shared Core Engine (Reused) Zero Rebuild
  • ANSI Z765 Standards Engine: Consistent GLA calculation between field & office.
  • Craftsman 2026 Valuation Index: Shared unit cost multipliers.
  • Gemini 3.0 Multimodal Pipeline: AI extraction for tax cards and blueprint PDFs.
  • UAD 3.6 / MISMO 3.4 Serializers: Standards-compliant appraisal export packages.
Office-Specific Layer (New) Under Construction
  • Multi-Project Pipeline Queue: Triage, sorting, and SLA assignment.
  • Split-Screen Auditor: Vector floor plan vs. county tax card side-by-side.
  • Automated Underwriting Rules (AUR): Instant discrepancy & risk flags.
  • Redline Adjustment Layer: Non-destructive underwriter cost overrides.

03 Proposed Office Functional Modules

Module A: Triage Pipeline & Inspection Queue

Underwriters manage portfolios, not single projects. The landing view provides an enterprise table with live search, SLA countdowns, and risk scores:

  • Configurable Filters: By Loan Officer, Loan Number, Property State, Inspector Name, and Urgency.
  • Automated Risk Score Badge: Immediate calculation based on square footage variance, C5/C6 condition ratings, and repair costs exceeding valuation thresholds.
  • Status Lifecycle: Submitted → In Desk Review → Flagged / Pending Revision → Approved → Declined.

Module B: Split-Screen Discrepancy Reconciliation Auditor

The signature desktop interface for appraisal compliance and fraud detection:

Pane 1: Field Inspection Reality
• Interactive vector CAD floor plan with room polygons.
• ANSI Gross Living Area (GLA) breakdown by level.
• Geotagged high-resolution defect and finish photos.
• Field appraiser condition ratings (C1 through C6).
Pane 2: Official Public Record / Blueprints
• Ingested County Assessor property card.
• Historical MLS listings and prior appraisal data.
• Permitted architectural blueprint drawings.
• Automated optical comparison highlighting variances.

Module C: Automated Underwriting Rules Engine (AUR)

Configurable risk checks that automatically flag loans requiring senior committee review:

Rule Trigger Threshold Condition Underwriting Action
GLA Variance Threshold Field GLA differs from County Tax Record by > 10.0% Flags potential unpermitted addition; triggers permit verification.
Cost-to-Cure Ratio Total repair estimate exceeds 15.0% of As-Is Value Mandatory repair escrow holdback requirement generated.
Severe Condition Class Any room/element graded Condition C5 or C6 Hard loan stop; requires engineer inspection or structural escrow.
Room Count Disparity Bedroom or full bath count does not match public record Requires underwriter reconciliation note before sign-off.
Below-Grade GLA Trap Walk-out basement included in Above-Grade GLA Automatic ANSI Z765 compliance violation flag; reclassifies square footage.

Module D: Non-Destructive Underwriter Adjustment Layer

Underwriters frequently reject contractor estimates or adjust labor rates. The system preserves the original field submission while providing a formal adjustment ledger:

  • Dual-Column Scope Accounting: Displays Field Estimate ($) alongside Underwriter Allowed ($).
  • Mandatory Reason Codes: Selecting an adjustment requires choosing an approved rationale (e.g., "Local trade index correction", "Cosmetic only—deferred maintenance", "Excessive contractor margin").
  • Automated Escrow Calculator: Automatically computes the lender holdback reserve (e.g., 110% to 150% of approved repairs).

Module E: Decision Memo & Secondary Market Compliance Exports

Replaces contractor bills with institutional lending documentation:

  • Underwriting Decision Memo (PDF): Formal, signed risk determination document itemizing approved repairs, required escrows, and loan conditions.
  • Fannie Mae Form 1004D / Completion Certificate Integration: Pre-populated inspection audit.
  • MISMO 3.4 / 3.6 Desk Review XML: Direct export for ingestion into Encompass, Black Knight, or proprietary Loan Origination Systems (LOS).

04 Field ↔ Office Collaboration & Re-Inspection Loop

When an underwriter identifies an unverified defect or unpermitted space, they need to request revisions without playing phone tag:

The 4-Step Re-Inspection Dispatch Loop
  1. Underwriter Pins Clarification Request: Underwriter clicks directly on a room (e.g., "Primary Bathroom") and requests: "Photograph subfloor water meter connection—suspected active leak."
  2. Field Notification: The field inspector's tablet receives a push notification and marks the project status as Action Required.
  3. Targeted Capture: The field app opens directly to the requested photo slot, requiring GPS and timestamp verification.
  4. Instant Office Resolution: The uploaded photo automatically satisfies the underwriting condition in the office view.

05 Stakeholder Requirements Questionnaire & Decision Matrix

Please review the 10 targeted questions below. Your input will directly shape the feature prioritization, data models, and integrations for Vis-Scope Office Edition.

Question 1 • Lending Environment & Core Audience
What is your primary lending / risk profile?
  • Private / Hard Money / Bridge Lending: Heavy focus on rehab budget accuracy, draw schedules, and After-Repair Value (ARV).
  • Conventional Mortgage (Fannie/Freddie GSE): Strict compliance with ANSI Z765 GLA, UAD 3.6, and Fannie Mae Form 1004/1004D.
  • Appraisal Management Company (AMC): High-volume desk reviews, quality control audits, and appraiser rebuttal management.
  • Insurance / Loss Mitigation: Pre-loss vs. post-loss scope of work and depreciation schedules.

Question 2 • Data Synchronization Topology
How should inspection packages travel between Field Inspectors and Underwriters?
  • Real-Time Cloud Central Database (Recommended): Field inspector taps "Submit", instantly appearing in the underwriter queue.
  • File-Based MeshEngine (.ZIP) Upload: Underwriters drag-and-drop the field bundle exported from the field app.
  • Hybrid / LOS Bridge: Field app exports to Loan Origination System (e.g. Encompass), which triggers Vis-Scope via webhook.
Stakeholder Notes:
Question 3 • Permitted vs. Unpermitted GLA Variance Tolerances
What variance between field-measured GLA and public records should trigger an automatic flag?
  • Strict (5.0%): Any discrepancy over 5% flags for permit verification.
  • Standard Industry Tolerance (10.0%): Discrepancies between 5% and 10% warn; > 10% hard flags.
  • Absolute Square Footage: Flag only if variance exceeds 200 sq. ft. regardless of percentage.
  • Custom Configurable: Set per loan product or client profile.
Stakeholder Notes:
Question 4 • Scope & Cost Adjustments Authority
What authority should underwriters have to alter contractor / inspector scopes?
  • Adjudication Layer Only (Preserve Original): Underwriter enters overrides alongside original field bids without editing original nodes.
  • Direct Editing with Audit History: Underwriter can directly modify quantities, with full versioned revision tracking.
  • Reject & Re-Submit Only: Underwriter cannot adjust numbers; must reject package back to field inspector with comments.
Stakeholder Notes:
Question 5 • Required Holdback / Escrow Calculations
How should repair holdbacks/escrows be determined automatically?
  • Flat Multiplier: Standard 150% of total estimated cost to cure.
  • Tiered by Trade Category: 110% for cosmetic trades (paint, carpet); 150% for mechanical/structural trades (HVAC, foundation).
  • Discretionary Underwriter Input: Underwriter types exact holdback dollar amount per item.
Stakeholder Notes:
Question 6 • Loan Origination System (LOS) Integrations
Which enterprise platforms need direct two-way API integration?
  • ICE Mortgage Technology (Encompass): Pipeline sync, eFolder document push, loan status updates.
  • Black Knight / Empower: Core loan servicing and underwriting feeds.
  • Custom REST / Webhooks: Proprietary internal loan origination or appraisal CRM.
  • Standalone First Phase: Export PDF/XML manually; add LOS API connections in Phase 2.
Stakeholder Notes:
Question 7 • Automated Defect Vision Verification (Gemini 3.0)
How should AI assist the underwriter during desk review?
  • Photo Defect Verification: Gemini 3.0 analyzes field photos to verify whether defect severity matches C4 vs C5/C6 claims.
  • Blueprint vs. CAD Cross-Check: AI compares blueprint dimensions to inspector's drawn vector canvas.
  • Permit Document Ingestion: AI scans uploaded permit PDFs and cross-references closed dates against renovation years.
Stakeholder Notes:
Question 8 • Role-Based Access Controls (RBAC) Hierarchy
What user permission levels are required within your organization?
  • Tier 1: Field Inspector: Create, draw, photograph, and submit projects; read-only on underwriting decisions.
  • Tier 2: Desk Reviewer / Credit Analyst: Audit, annotate, apply rules, calculate holdbacks, recommend sign-off.
  • Tier 3: Senior Underwriting Manager / Chief Credit Officer: Final sign-off, override hard loan-stops, approve policy exceptions.
  • Tier 4: Enterprise Admin: Manage fee multipliers, LOS API keys, user seats, and billing.
